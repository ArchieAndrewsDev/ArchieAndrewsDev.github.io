SLAVE KNIGHT GAEL - DEVOPS

A cinematic project I provided version control setup and support for.

For most of 2026 I looked after version control for a fan-made cinematic based on Dark Souls 3. The brief was for something a team of five could work on together, handling an Unreal project with lots of large art assets. My own goals were to keep costs as low as possible while preventing data loss.

The setup

In January I set up an Azure DevOps organisation to host the git repo. It wasn’t the perfect solution, but it was free for our five users, with a 250 GB repo limit that included LFS files. Managing it was simple, and getting the artists and animators onto the Fork git client made everything straightforward for them and for me.

Fork is what I use day to day for game engine work and beyond. In my opinion it’s the best git client out there: quick, stable, friendly for people who don’t know git well, and affordable ($59.99 for a lifetime licence, with a free evaluation). Not and ad I just really like this client!

Working with Jonjo, Sophie, Antonio, Tammy and Jean was a dream. I wrote some basic documentation for anyone new to git, then heard very little from them as they powered through their work, committing regularly to the repo.

Backups

I run my own Gitea server at home with plenty of storage for a project like this, and I did test it with a few people when I started looking at solutions. In the end I decided against it, because I needed the repo available 24/7, which I couldn’t guarantee with my Cornish home internet :( As a middle ground, I synced the repo to my Gitea server so I had a full copy with its history, and that copy went into my weekly off-site backup.

What I’d do next time

If I worked on a project like this again, I’d be keen to automate regular renders, so people like Jonjo could review changes without being at their workstation with the project pulled and open. I’m thinking a git workflow that renders shots at low resolution and frame rate on each commit, or at the end of the day for larger shots, with a manual trigger for a full-quality render. Depending on costs I would aim to keep the render server on cloud in order to scale quickly with the project. I'm going to play around with this on my home server but might need something a little faster than the 1050 in there at the moment.

It’s been great working with a team of professionals and watching this project develop over the months I was involved. Thank you again, Jonjo and co, for letting me play a small part in it!

PREFAB BRUSH+

← Back

In 2015 I created a tool to fill a need I had for a project. I wanted something that could scatter prefabs with random transform values across any collider surface. The tool I built for this would go on to become PrefabBrush+ which has since sold 3,845 copies as of December 2025.

Unity Asset Store

PrefabBrush+ is my favorite child. It was one of the first projects I finished and one of the very few that made it out into the wild. It's been fun to maintain the project over the year and expand on its features to try and keep my little share of the market.

Most recently I pushed the 1.4.x update for the 2025 new years sale. This update was a bit of a feature creep. I intended to add one new feature and do a little spring cleaning, in the end I did a partial redesign of the UI, merging many of the options into one of two categories (modifiers and filters) and I added a new filter for terrain textures.

PrefabBrush+ is over 10 years old now and the code has gone through a lot of changes. Despite that it still suffers from being a tool made by someone with limited experience in tools and games development. One thing I'm especially proud of is that PrefabBrush+ works with every version of Unity since Unity 5.

If any can find a version where it doesn't work please let me know though ;)

I do plan on doing a full rewrite of PrefabBrush+ as a new tool for the Asset Store. No idea when I will get around to it though. I need to convince my self it's going to be really easy and fast even though it will be slow and a lot of work :D

Thank you to anyone who has bought PrefabBrush+ over the past 10 years, I hope it has helped you make the games you wanted to make.

Course Page

I can split the job I have at Falmouth Uni into 2 sections, games and everything else. On the games front I get the opportunity to support hundreds of students on their team projects across all 4 years of courses. More recently I have had the chance to steer the direction of our 1st year teaching by taking lead on our module that teaches the basics of Unity to all disciplines. I get to help maintain and run our Github Enterprise server that serves the entire department. One of my main responsibilities is the teaching and support of version control. Through the years I have seen some amazing ways for students to break repos in git and written documentation to try and mitigate those situations as much as possible.

The other half of the job is a mixed bag of what ever is needed at that time of year. One area of teaching that I found my self in is the Esports course. I lead the module that introduces the students to our broadcast kit such as the Tricaster Elite 2.

The other key task I am involved with is our end of year EXPO show. Falmouth Games Academy goes all out for this compared to other university end of year shows. We try to emulate an small expo event where the student teams can show off their games to industry guests and the public.

I often take lead on AV for these events producing a series of talks for a stage. Like all the other technicians I also help haul 100s of computers, desks and chairs to the event space.

I enjoy working at Falmouth, education is a passion of mine and being able to steer the direction of teaching and supporting people starting their journey into games is very rewarding.
