PREFAB BRUSH+

← Back

In 2015 I created a tool to fill a need I had for a project. I wanted something that could scatter prefabs with random transform values across any collider surface. The tool I built for this would go on to become PrefabBrush+ which has since sold 3,845 copies as of December 2025.

Unity Asset Store

PrefabBrush+ is my favorite child. It was one of the first projects I finished and one of the very few that made it out into the wild. It's been fun to maintain the project over the years and expand on its features to try and keep my little share of the market.

Most recently I pushed the 1.4.x update for the 2025 new years sale. This update was a bit of a feature creep. I intended to add one new feature and do a little spring cleaning, in the end I did a partial redesign of the UI, merging many of the options into one of two categories (modifiers and filters) and I added a new filter for terrain textures.

PrefabBrush+ is over 10 years old now and the code has gone through a lot of changes. Despite that it still suffers from being a tool made by someone with limited experience in tools and games development. One thing I'm especially proud of is that PrefabBrush+ works with every version of Unity since Unity 5.

If anyone can find a version where it doesn't work please let me know though ;)

I do plan on doing a full rewrite of PrefabBrush+ as a new tool for the Asset Store. No idea when I will get around to it though. I need to convince myself it's going to be really easy and fast even though it will be slow and a lot of work :D

Thank you to anyone who has bought PrefabBrush+ over the past 10 years, I hope it has helped you make the games you wanted to make.

ZURVIVE

← Back

Over the 2020 lock-down I began an ambitious project with a friend. We wanted to attempt creating a large survival game. The intention of the project was to identify what tools and problems we would need to build a game of this nature and use this as an opportunity to learn.

Zurvive was never going to get made, we knew this going in but I find you learn more from a project if you go in with undeserved confidence and a hefty spoonful of ignorance. The goal of this project was to learn, everything we made we intended to throw out. We wanted to make mistakes while we had the time locked up in our house.

For me this project was actually a chance to step away from programming and try my hand at some more design tasks. Exploration in games is an area that interests me so the idea of learning more on how to design large worlds was exciting. I started off my expedition into design by... writing a ton of code.

We had decided to use Unity for this project mainly as we had a wealth of experience between us and wanted to spend our time building on that. The issue with Unity is it can be a blank slate for your project. While some engines like Unreal have a great collection of systems you can jump into, Unity requires you to purchase things from the Asset Store or build them yourself.

I began building little diorama scenes in order to find gaps in Unity's tools and build solutions for them. I experimented with using Addressables to load chunks of a scene in and out of memory asynchronously. I built a tile tool that would let you duplicate a selected tillable object in 4 directions. I also used PrefabBrush+ to build some test forests.

I also spent a lot of time iterating over a character controller and trying to get something that felt like it would belong in the games we were taking inspiration from.

In the end we both became too busy to work on this project and moved onto other projects.

If I could have infinite time and money I would make Zurvive. There is so much about this sort of project that excites me.

FALMOUTH UNIVERSITY

← Back

Since October 2019 I have been working as a technician at Falmouth University's Games Academy. My role has been to support students and staff in use of games development tools like Unity and Git. I also help maintain facilities, equipment and the DevOps for the departments of roughly 900 students. The reality is though I wear a shop full of hats in this role across the course of the academic year.

Games Academy

I can split the job I have at Falmouth Uni into 2 sections, games and everything else.

On the games front I get the opportunity to support hundreds of students on their team projects across all 4 years of courses. More recently I have had the chance to steer the direction of our 1st year teaching by taking lead on our module that teaches the basics of Unity to all disciplines. I get to help maintain and run our GitHub Enterprise server that serves the entire department. One of my main responsibilities is the teaching and support of version control. Through the years I have seen some amazing ways for students to break repos in git and written documentation to try and mitigate those situations as much as possible.

The other half of the job is a mixed bag of whatever is needed at that time of year. One area of teaching that I found myself in is the Esports course. I lead the module that introduces the students to our broadcast kit such as the Tricaster Elite 2.

The other key task I am involved with is our end of year EXPO show. Falmouth Games Academy goes all out for this compared to other university end of year shows. We try to emulate a small expo event where the student teams can show off their games to industry guests and the public.

I often take lead on AV for these events producing a series of talks for a stage. Like all the other technicians I also help haul 100s of computers, desks and chairs to the event space.

I enjoy working at Falmouth, education is a passion of mine and being able to steer the direction of teaching and supporting people starting their journey into games is very rewarding.
